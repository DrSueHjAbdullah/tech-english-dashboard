Grammar

Passive Voice in Technical Writing

Used to focus on the process rather than the agent. E.g., "The data was collected using sensors."

Vocabulary

Key Technical Verbs

analyse, implement, configure, integrate, validate, optimise, deploy, simulate.

Writing

Abstract Structure

Background → Objective → Method → Results → Conclusion. Keep under 250 words.

Pronunciation

Stress in Technical Terms

SYS-tem, pro-CESS, CON-text, a-NAL-y-sis — stress shifts often when adding suffixes.

TechEnglish — Grammar Notes
8 grammar topics for technical communication
Grammar

1. Modal Verbs in Technical Context

Must — compulsory requirement: "Technicians must wear safety equipment."
Should — advice: "You should inspect the machine regularly."
May — possibility/permission: "The device may overheat."
Can — ability: "This sensor can detect temperature changes."

Grammar

2. Passive Voice in Technical Writing

Structure: Subject + be + past participle.
Active: "The technician installed the software."
Passive: "The software was installed."
Present: "The machine is inspected daily."
Past: "The data was collected yesterday."
Future: "The system will be tested tomorrow."

Grammar

3. Present Simple & Present Continuous

Present simple — facts, routines, procedures:
"Water boils at 100°C." / "The system records temperature."
Present continuous — ongoing actions:
"The technician is checking the circuit."
"The machine is operating at reduced speed."

Grammar

4. Past Simple in Technical Reports

Used in methodology & results sections.
Positive: "The team conducted three experiments."
Negative: "The sensor did not detect the signal."
Question: "Did the equipment operate correctly?"
Note: use base verb after did/did not.

Grammar

5. Conditional Sentences for Safety

Zero: "If metal gets hot, it expands."
First: "If the temperature rises, the system will shut down."
Second: "If we had a larger battery, the device would operate longer."
Note: Do not use will in the if-clause (first conditional).

Grammar

6. Subject–Verb Agreement

Singular: "The sensor detects movement."
Plural: "The sensors detect movement."
Uncountable: "The equipment is ready."
Each/every: "Each component requires inspection."
Incorrect: "The results shows…" → Correct: "The results show…"

Grammar

7. Comparatives & Superlatives

Comparative: faster than, more durable than.
"Device A is faster than Device B."
Superlative: the fastest, the most efficient.
"This is the most efficient method."
Irregular: good → better → best.

Grammar

8. Articles & Quantifiers

A/an — new item: "A sensor measures temperature."
The — specific item: "The sensor is connected."
No article — general: "Copper conducts electricity."
Many/few (countable) · Much/little (uncountable).
Note: equipment, information, research — uncountable.
